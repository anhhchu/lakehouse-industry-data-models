# Title Examination and Underwriting Policy

## Evidence

Every material title finding must cite one or more evidence records. Evidence includes recorded instruments, public-record index entries, court records, tax records, maps, prior policies, affidavits, and approved external reports. The model must preserve the evidence source, retrieval date, jurisdiction, document identifiers, and image or document reference.

## Finding Classification

A finding is an examiner's conclusion about evidence. Findings are classified as requirement, exception, informational item, cleared item, or underwriting referral. Classification can change through review, but each prior classification must remain historically available.

## Requirements and Exceptions

A requirement is a condition to be satisfied, waived, or otherwise dispositioned before closing or issuance. An exception is a matter excluded from coverage. The same evidence can support both a requirement and a proposed exception, but the requirement and exception remain separate records with separate lifecycles.

## Curative Work

Curative actions include obtaining releases, subordinations, corrective instruments, affidavits, probate documentation, marital-status evidence, entity authority documentation, payoff confirmation, and underwriting approval. A curative action can address multiple findings, and a finding can require multiple actions.

## Underwriting Decisions

Underwriting decisions record the issue, requested approval, authority level, decision, conditions, deciding underwriter, decision timestamp, and supporting evidence. Conditions imposed by an underwriter are not considered satisfied until evidence of satisfaction is reviewed.

## Temporal Rules

- Preserve instrument execution, effective, recording, and retrieval dates separately.
- Represent ownership as time-bounded interests supported by evidence.
- Do not infer legal priority solely from ingestion order.
- Do not delete a resolved defect, satisfied requirement, or superseded decision.
