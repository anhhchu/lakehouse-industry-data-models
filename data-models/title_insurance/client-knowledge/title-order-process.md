# Title Order Process

## Intake

An order is opened for a purchase, refinance, home-equity, construction, or commercial transaction. Intake records requested products, expected closing date, property, parcels, parties, lender and loan when applicable, originating channel, responsible branch or agent, and service-level commitments.

One order can concern multiple parcels. A parcel can participate in multiple orders over time. Orders can be amended, cancelled, reopened, transferred, or split. Status history must be append-only for material transitions.

## Search

Operations request a search from an internal title plant or approved search vendor. A search package can contain deeds, mortgages, deeds of trust, liens, judgments, easements, restrictions, taxes, probate records, court records, maps, and supporting images. Search updates and bring-downs remain linked to the original package.

## Examination

An examiner reviews evidence, establishes the apparent chain of title, identifies findings, and classifies each finding. Examination can be referred to an underwriter when authority limits or risk conditions require approval.

## Commitment

A commitment is issued after examination and can be revised. It identifies the proposed insureds and estate, requirements to be satisfied, and exceptions from coverage. A commitment is not a policy.

## Closing and Recording

After conditions are satisfied and approvals obtained, the transaction closes. Recordable documents are submitted to the recording jurisdiction. Rejected documents are corrected and resubmitted. Recording confirmation includes the jurisdiction-assigned instrument number or book and page.

## Policy Issuance

Owner and lender policies are issued after required recording confirmation and post-closing review. The first commitment, revised commitments, pro formas, and final policies remain separately identifiable.

## Demonstration Edge Cases

- One order covers three parcels in two recording jurisdictions.
- A commitment is revised after a new judgment appears in a bring-down search.
- A deed is rejected for an acknowledgment defect and later resubmitted.
- A closing is completed, but the lender policy is delayed by a missing recorded assignment.
- An order is cancelled after search work is completed and fees have been incurred.
